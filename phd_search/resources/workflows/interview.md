# /interview — research opportunity interview preparation and mock interview

1. Identify the exact tracked application, opportunity family and interview stage, date/timezone, format, panel, talk/task and actual assessment criteria. Internships may assess a feasible project contribution; schools may assess prerequisites or learning goals and often have no interview. Do not invent a doctoral proposal defence or an interview requirement. `./roost application show APP_ID` provides events. Schedule with `application event --kind interview_scheduled --file event.json` using an offset-aware `at` timestamp and source note.
2. Use the immutable submitted archive, including the exact CV, statements/proposal, posting and previous feedback. `./roost interview APP_ID` creates a preparation file from this basis. If not submitted, state that the packet is based on current drafts. Never prepare the candidate to defend achievements only present in a later revision.
3. Research panel identity, relevant recent work and current project/programme structure from primary sources. Verify exact references and what was read. Avoid speculation about personality or private assessment criteria.
4. Develop a 60-second and 3-minute research pitch grounded in the submitted agenda: question, motivation, preparation, proposed contribution, approach, limitations and fit. Adapt to the discipline; some interviews emphasise textual interpretation, practice, a portfolio, theory, clinical reasoning or professional expertise rather than experiments.
5. Prepare concrete examples of independent thinking, methodological choice, collaboration, challenge, ambiguous/negative results and learning. Separate the candidate's individual contribution from group results. Use STAR where it clarifies experience; do not force every scholarly answer into that form. Build honest bridge answers for genuine gaps.
6. Practice explaining one relevant output deeply: question, evidence/material, method and alternatives, result, limitations, ethics/access and future work. For a proposal discuss feasibility, project scope, contingency and training. Distinguish promises from hypotheses and planned studies.
7. Prepare questions about supervision/feedback, doctoral progression, resources, funding duration/fees, teaching, research freedom, access, publication/practice expectations and wellbeing based on actual unknowns. Do not ask generic questions whose answers are already explicit.
8. In mock mode ask one question at a time, wait for the answer, then offer concise evidence-based feedback and a revision. Do not invent the user's answers. Cover scientific, methodological, programme-fit and practical questions.
9. Record preparation, feedback and next actions. After an actual interview, use `/outcome` for stage/status and a thank-you draft if requested. Do not mark an interview completed or a follow-up sent because the draft exists.
